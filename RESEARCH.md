# Learning Sudoku inside a fly-connectome simulation

Source review: 2026-09-10. The chosen priority is learning inside the fly network
with a fixed action decoder. This document records a code audit and a proposed
experiment, not training results. Upstream full-network experiments were not
rerun during this review.

Implementation update: subsequent local experiments completed assisted visual
cue learning (Step 1) and familiar-symbol comparison (Step 2). The latter uses
fixed template recognition and sensory pooling, with learned responses stored
in existing fly synapses through the unchanged local rule. The
[controlled result and its limits](experiments/level-02/005-controlled-replication/README.md)
are recorded separately from this historical source audit. Sudoku solving
remains a later stage.

## The runnable training loop in the public repositories

The [MaleCNS dataset](https://male-cns.janelia.org/) supplies reconstructed
anatomical connectivity. It does not supply calibrated executable physiology or
a pretrained game-playing policy. Both projects retain 166,700 neurons and
25,582,938 directed connections. Those edges aggregate 124,177,617 anatomical
synaptic contacts; connections and contacts are different counts.

Their approximate leaky integrate-and-fire neurons accumulate input, leak toward
rest, and emit spikes at a threshold. Initial connection magnitude is anatomical
contact count multiplied by a scale factor; transmitter annotations supply a
coarse excitatory/inhibitory sign. The timestep is 0.1 ms. This is a model based
on reconstructed wiring, not a complete reconstruction of a living fly's brain.
See [graph preparation](https://github.com/nftechie/stonkfly/blob/78ef3e05ab0fa086032098558d893667068944a0/stonkfly/neural/prepare.py)
and [neural kernel](https://github.com/nftechie/stonkfly/blob/78ef3e05ab0fa086032098558d893667068944a0/stonkfly/neural/kernel.cpp).

The sensory adapter turns RGB images into drive for 3,335 R1–R6 brightness cells
and 811 R8 color cells. Screen positions and color responses are engineered
proxies. The network must produce a usable internal representation from that
input; displaying a digit does not establish that it recognizes that digit.
See [Doomfly's sensory protocol](https://github.com/nftechie/doomfly/blob/71ecf53d78eaffaf1a57ed7b0ccf5d458abc9f33/docs/doom-live-training.md).

Doomfly reads DNp20 right-minus-left activity for turning and DNpe017 for movement
and firing. Stonkfly reads those populations as buy/sell/hold: a difference of at
least +2 Hz or at most −2 Hz, with an action-gating spike, selects buy or sell.
These meanings were assigned by the developers. There are no biological
"buy neurons." See [Doomfly's decoder](https://github.com/nftechie/doomfly/blob/71ecf53d78eaffaf1a57ed7b0ccf5d458abc9f33/doom/engine.py#L102-L127)
and [Stonkfly's decoder](https://github.com/nftechie/stonkfly/blob/78ef3e05ab0fa086032098558d893667068944a0/stonkfly/neural/controller.py#L11-L46).

The environment schedules reinforcement after an outcome:

- Doomfly: nonfatal damage schedules 200 ms of artificial stimulation to two
  PPL101 dopamine cells. Fatal damage does not receive this pulse. Neural state
  and memory normally persist across rounds.
- Stonkfly: a portfolio-value change of at least +0.01 USDC stimulates 15 PAM11
  cells; at most −0.01 USDC stimulates two PPL101 cells. These are binary pulses,
  not currents proportional to profit. Price movement while holding an asset
  can trigger them, so the latest trade need not have caused the feedback.

Sources: [Doomfly reinforcement code](https://github.com/nftechie/doomfly/blob/71ecf53d78eaffaf1a57ed7b0ccf5d458abc9f33/doom/training.py#L34-L67),
[Stonkfly reinforcement code](https://github.com/nftechie/stonkfly/blob/78ef3e05ab0fa086032098558d893667068944a0/stonkfly/reinforcement.py),
and [Stonkfly model](https://github.com/nftechie/stonkfly/blob/78ef3e05ab0fa086032098558d893667068944a0/docs/model.md).

## What changes during training

Kenyon cells (KCs) are neurons in the fly's mushroom body; MBONs are mushroom-body
output neurons; DANs are dopamine neurons. The candidate learning mechanism
modifies selected existing KC→MBON synaptic strengths using recent KC and DAN
activity. The broader graph and the action decoder remain fixed.

In simplified notation, the active rule is:

```text
K, D       = current KC and dopamine-neuron firing rates
Kbar, Dbar = their one-second exponentially filtered histories
q          = eta * (K * (Gᵀ Dbar) - (Gᵀ D) * Kbar)
du/dt      = q - u / 1800 seconds
dw/dt      = (u - w) / 0.05 seconds
W          = W_initial * (1 + w)
```

G distributes dopamine modulation using anatomical contact fractions. The
deviation states u and w are clipped to [−0.9, 1], so efficacies remain between
0.1× and 2× their initial values. Rates are measured in bins no longer than
10 ms; eta defaults to 0.001. Doomfly v6 subtracts a calibrated DAN baseline;
Stonkfly's default DAN baseline subtraction is zero.

Recent KC activity followed by dopamine tends to weaken eligible connections;
the reverse timing can strengthen them. "Reward" therefore does not simply
mean increasing every useful weight. Effects on actions depend on the downstream
circuit. This is an adaptation of an associative-memory model, with no policy
gradient, backpropagation, or explicit record of which game action deserves credit.

Sources: [Stonkfly active rule](https://github.com/nftechie/stonkfly/blob/78ef3e05ab0fa086032098558d893667068944a0/stonkfly/neural/rule.py),
[Stonkfly execution and edge writes](https://github.com/nftechie/stonkfly/blob/78ef3e05ab0fa086032098558d893667068944a0/stonkfly/neural/brain.py#L300-L352),
[Doomfly baseline handling](https://github.com/nftechie/doomfly/blob/71ecf53d78eaffaf1a57ed7b0ccf5d458abc9f33/doom_learning_v6/brain.py#L115-L130),
and [Huang, Luo et al. (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07819-w).

An audit trap: the native kernel retains an older learning branch. The current
Python loop explicitly disables that branch and applies the rule above instead.

Weight changes are only one link in the evidence chain. Doomfly's small v6
survival pilot found a harmful effect on one held-out start, and erasing memory
restored the frozen baseline's trajectory. This demonstrates that memory could
change behavior in that run, not that it improved behavior. See the
[recorded iteration results](https://github.com/nftechie/doomfly/blob/71ecf53d78eaffaf1a57ed7b0ccf5d458abc9f33/docs/doom-learning-iteration-log.md#L181-L203).

## Proposed Sudoku experiment

Everything below is a proposed design, not an upstream capability or a prediction
that this rule will succeed. Start with the documented full graph and a fixed
sensory/action interface. Stonkfly's neural subsystem supplies both reinforcement
compartments; Doomfly supplies more extensive experimental controls. Reuse their
neural work with provenance rather than including trading or game services.

### First establish a learnable interface

1. Present two simple, brightness-matched visual cues. Check that they produce
   distinguishable, repeatable activity in the candidate memory circuit.
   Activity should return toward baseline between trials rather than remain
   saturated or silent.
2. Measure whether changes within the permitted KC→MBON edges can influence the
   proposed fixed output population. A connection path alone is insufficient;
   use paired interventions from the same initial state.
3. Run a cue-conditioning and reversal task with fixed accept/reject decoding.
   Demonstrate a changed behavioral preference that survives removal of reward.
   If this fails, diagnose the representation, dynamics, or motor coupling before
   making the task more complex.

Use DNp20 right-minus-left activity as an initial engineered binary interface,
with a fixed observation window and declared threshold. Count a neutral output
as a timeout, not a secretly correct choice. If a different neuron readout is
needed, document it as a new model variant and evaluate it separately. Do not
fit the readout to the final Sudoku test set.

### Progress from symbols to puzzles

Numbering note: this original table starts after cue calibration. The implemented
project calls cue learning **Step 1** and the first row below, symbol comparison,
**Step 2**. The following one-blank Sudoku task is not the current Step 2 goal.

| Stage | Task | What passing it would establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A short row using four symbols, plus a proposed symbol: does it repeat one already present? | Learned symbol comparison on new arrangements. |
| 2 | A 4×4 Sudoku with one blank and one proposed digit: accept or reject it. | Correct missing-symbol judgments on unseen boards. This alone does not require all Sudoku rules. |
| 3 | Partial 4×4 boards with candidates that isolate row, column, and box conflicts. | Sensitivity to each constraint rather than a single shortcut. |
| 4 | Complete multi-blank 4×4 puzzles with a fixed candidate scan and an explicit undo action. | Sequential puzzle completion under the declared interface and move budget. |
| 5 | Larger 9×9 tasks with increasing numbers of blanks and harder deductions. | Transfer to standard Sudoku, only if measured. |

Initially render a large, high-contrast board, highlighted target cell, and
proposed digit into the fixed visual input. Balance digit identity, position,
correctness, and overall brightness so none predicts reward alone. If retinal
input fails, a fixed symbolic stimulation scheme can be a separate experiment;
it would change the claim from visual play to symbolic input through an
engineered interface. It must not encode legal candidates or solution hints.

For the binary task, each trial commits one decision, then receives a short
correct/incorrect teaching pulse. Keep that outcome close to the decision in
simulated time and separate the next trial so its stimulus is not accidentally
paired with the previous reward. Train both classes evenly; also report results
over every candidate digit, since an always-reject policy can otherwise look good.

A known solution may grade training trials. It must never select the agent's
action, enter its observation, or be used to correct its output. Reward using a
hidden solution is supervised teaching delivered through dopamine; label it as
such and use uniquely solvable puzzles. Stage 3 instead labels immediate rule
violations: include matched examples where the proposed digit appears elsewhere
outside the target's row, column, and box, so a global duplicate detector fails.
The biological meaning and useful behavioral effect of the chosen reward
compartments still need to be established experimentally.

For full puzzles, scan cells and all digits in a fixed or independently seeded
order, with no solver selecting promising cells or filtering candidates. The fly
accepts or skips each option; include undo as another scanned option so the same
fixed binary decoder can request it. This is an assisted interface and must be
described as such. A locally legal placement can still make a puzzle
unsolvable; easy conflict checks cannot substitute for sequential reasoning.

### What would count as success

- Split base puzzles before producing augmented copies; keep equivalent copies
  together. Hold out arrangements and record the limited structural variety of
  4×4 boards. Do not infer broad Sudoku generalization from that small domain.
- Compare learning against the same frozen initial network, shuffled/yoked
  teaching signals, and simple constant/random choices under equal budgets.
- Use several independently seeded runs and report every run, uncertainty, and
  predeclared progression criteria. Trials within one run are not independent
  training replicates.
- During evaluation, freeze weights, remove teaching pulses and per-move
  correctness feedback, and standardize transient neural state. Freezing must
  stop both learning and passive u/w relaxation and edge writes; setting eta to
  zero alone is insufficient. For full-board
  evaluation, commit the agent's choices without an oracle rejecting wrong
  moves; score the final board separately by checking all Sudoku constraints
  and preservation of the givens, not identity with one stored answer.
- Check retention and repeat evaluation with learned weights erased while
  holding the electrical starting state fixed and resetting KC/DAN activity
  traces in both arms. Erase the corresponding u/w memory deviations too, so
  latent memory cannot restore the old weights. Improvement should depend on
  those learned connections.
- Report balanced candidate accuracy, precision/recall, timeouts, complete-board
  solve rate, move budget, and computation time. Changing weights and filling
  cells do not by themselves establish learning.

The first useful result is a retained improvement on unseen small tasks caused
by internal plasticity. A full 9×9 solver remains an open research objective.
There is presently no justified training-time estimate: benchmark one real
stimulus/trial before budgeting longer full-graph experiments.

## Implementation boundary

The smallest first implementation should be one primary experiment file using
an explicitly pinned upstream neural implementation, plus run instructions. It
should generate tiny tasks, render stimuli, execute neural trials, deliver
training feedback, and write enough results to reproduce the controls above.
Do not build a dashboard or introduce an external trained policy at this stage.

Before reusing upstream code, preserve its MIT notices and the separate
[MaleCNS data attribution](https://male-cns.janelia.org/download/). Neural runtime
dependencies and large connectome downloads are needed for an actual full-graph
run; none were installed or downloaded into this project during this review.
